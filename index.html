The instrument

Godometer reads ten of Earth's religious traditions from the outside: what kind of thing each god is, how much room it leaves a human being, and how its doctrine sits beside eleven modern human values.

Method

Perspective — hypothetical atheistic alien analysis of religious theology.

Basis — theological compatibility, not current social practice, government policy, or the behaviour of followers.

The four verdicts

  • Fully allowed — broadly compatible with the theology and not subject to a major doctrinal restriction.
  • Partially allowed — allowed with restrictions, conditional, read differently by major schools, or only partly accepted.
  • Not allowed — mainstream or classical theology considers it religiously or morally illegitimate.
  • Not applicable — the value assumes a theological structure the tradition does not contain, or a historical setting it did not survive into.

Reading the numbers

Divine authority and human autonomy are the tribunal's calibration of the god-model and autonomy findings, on a 0–100 scale, so the traditions can be charted against each other.

Values match is computed directly from the verdicts: fully allowed 100, partially allowed 50, not allowed 0, averaged across all applicable values.

Godometer · a hypothetical exercise in comparative theology, written from an imagined outside perspective. It describes doctrine, not people. No tradition here is being scored as better or worse than another — only as more or less structurally compatible with a specific list of modern values. Artwork is illustrative; several traditions represented here hold that their god may not be depicted at all.
